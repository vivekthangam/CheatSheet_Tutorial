# 1. The Anatomy of Phrasal Verbs: Why They Confuse Non-Native Speakers

A **Phrasal Verb** is formed by combining a common verb (like *look, bring, take, turn, call*) with a preposition or adverb particle (like *into, up, off, out, down*).

When combined, the words create an entirely new, non-literal meaning:

```
┌────────────┐   ┌─────────────────┐   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ BASE VERB  │ + │   PREPOSITION   │ = │   NEW PHRASAL VERB & MEANING            │
├────────────┤   ├─────────────────┤   ├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Look       │ + │ Into            │ = │ Investigate (ஆராய்ந்து பார்த்தல்)       │
│ Figure     │ + │ Out             │ = │ Solve / Understand (யோசித்து வழி அறிதல்)│
│ Call       │ + │ Off             │ = │ Cancel (ரத்து செய்தல்)                   │
│ Bring      │ + │ Up              │ = │ Mention in speech (பேச்சில் கொண்டுவருதல்) │
│ Wrap       │ + │ Up              │ = │ Conclude / Finish (முடித்துக் கொள்ளுதல்) │
└────────────┘   └─────────────────┘   └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> ⚠️ **Why Native Speakers Use Them Constantly**: Native English speakers use phrasal verbs in over **80% of daily speech** and informal work discussions. Saying *"We will investigate the server error"* sounds very stiff and robotic. Saying *"We are looking into the server error"* sounds natural, collaborative, and fluent!

# 2. Separable vs. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs (The Golden Grammar Rule)

Phrasal verbs follow two distinct structural patterns. Getting this wrong sounds jarring to international peers:

### Pattern 1: Separable Phrasal Verbs
The object can sit **between** the verb and the particle, or **after** the particle. However, if the object is a **pronoun** (*it, him, her, them*), it **MUST** go in the middle!

- ✅ *"Please **turn off** the monitor."*
- ✅ *"Please **turn** the monitor **off**."*
- ✅ *"Please **turn it off**."* (With pronoun: middle only!)
- ❌ *"Please turn off it."* (Unnatural English!)

### Pattern 2: Inseparable Phrasal Verbs
The verb and preposition can **never** be separated by an object or pronoun:

- ✅ *"I **ran into** Suresh at the cafeteria."*
- ✅ *"I **ran into** him."*
- ❌ *"I ran Suresh into."* (Grammatically incorrect!)

## Category A: Investigating, Solving & Clarifying (1–15)

---

### 1. **Look into** (*Inseparable*)
- **Definition**: To investigate, inspect, or examine the details of a problem.
- **Tamil Meaning**: ஒரு பிரச்சனை அல்லது விஷயத்தை ஆழமாக விசாரித்து ஆராய்தல்.
- 💡 *Daily Life*: *"I'll look into nearby rental flats in Velachery this Saturday."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"Thanks for alerting us about the 504 gateway timeout; our backend squad is looking into the proxy logs right now."*
- 🤝 *Customer Support*: *"We have logged your ticket and our billing department will look into the duplicate transaction today."*

---

### 2. **Figure out** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To solve a complex problem or discover the truth through reasoning and experimentation.
- **Tamil Meaning**: யோசித்து அல்லது முயற்சி செய்து ஒரு விஷயத்திற்கு தீர்வு அல்லது விடை காணுதல்.
- 💡 *Daily Life*: *"We need to figure out how to fit three large bags into this auto-rickshaw."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"It took the team nearly three hours to figure out why the Docker container was unable to resolve external DNS."*
- 🤝 *Career*: *"Don't stress if your long-term career path isn't clear right now; you'll figure it out as you build experience."*

---

### 3. **Drill down** (*Inseparable*)
- **Definition**: To examine data or an issue at a much deeper, granular level of detail.
- **Tamil Meaning**: மேலோட்டமாக பார்க்காமல் நுணுக்கமான விவரங்களில் இறங்கி ஆராய்தல்.
- 💡 *Finance*: *"Let's drill down into our monthly UPI expenses to see where our discretionary money goes."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"Let's drill down into the PostgreSQL slow query logs to identify which table joins are causing the CPU spike."*
- 🤝 *Analytics*: *"The dashboard enables stakeholders to drill down by country, region, and operating system."*

---

### 4. **Rule out** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To eliminate an option or cause from consideration because it has proven irrelevant.
- **Tamil Meaning**: இது காரணமாக இருக்க முடியாது என்று முடிவு செய்து ஒதுக்குதல்.
- 💡 *Healthcare*: *"The doctor ordered a chest X-ray to rule out viral pneumonia."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"We inspected the AWS NAT Gateway logs and ruled out network bandwidth throttling as the bottleneck."*
- 🤝 *Architecture*: *"We haven't ruled out using MongoDB, but Postgres is currently our top architectural choice."*

---

### 5. **Weed out** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To identify and remove inferior, undesirable, or corrupt elements from a group.
- **Tamil Meaning**: களை எடுப்பது போல தேவையற்ற அல்லது தரமற்றவற்றை நீக்குதல்.
- 💡 *Daily Life*: *"I spent Sunday morning weeding out clothes I haven't worn in three years."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"Automated coding assessments help talent acquisition teams weed out candidates who lack basic DSA foundations."*
- 💻 *Code Quality*: *"Our SonarQube static code scanner weeds out cyclic dependencies and unhandled null exceptions."*

---

### 6. **Iron out** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To resolve minor difficulties, edge-case bugs, or interpersonal disagreements smoothly.
- **Tamil Meaning**: ஆடையில் உள்ள சுருக்கத்தை தேய்ப்பது போல சிறு சிக்கல்களை பேசி சமரசம் செய்தல்.
- 💡 *Personal*: *"The newly married couple spent their first months ironing out their household budget habits."*
- 💻 *IT Workplace*: *"We need a quick 15-minute huddle with the Product Owner to iron out the acceptance criteria for guest checkout."*
- 🤝 *Release*: *"Our QA team spent the final sprint days ironing out minor UI alignment bugs before the public launch."*

### 7. **Spell out** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To explain something in exhaustive, crystal-clear, step-by-step detail.
- **Tamil Meaning**: எதையும் மறைக்காமல் குழந்தைக்கும் புரியும் அளவுக்கு மிகத் தெளிவாக விளக்குதல்.

### 8. **Narrow down** (*Separable*)

### 13. **Brush up on** (*Inseparable*)
- **Definition**: To refresh one's existing knowledge or skills in an area that hasn't been practiced recently.

### 14. **Sift through** (*Inseparable*)
- **Definition**: To examine a large volume of data, logs, or items to find what is useful or relevant.

### 17. **Put off** (*Separable*)
- **Definition**: To postpone or delay something until a later time or date.
- **Tamil Meaning**: ஒரு வேலையை செய்யாமல் பிற்காலத்திற்கு தள்ளிப்போடுதல்.

# 5. How to Eliminate "Tanglish" Phrases: The 25 Most Common Mistakes

Indian speakers frequently translate Tamil idiomatic phrasing directly into English. Study this correction chart to sound globally polished:

| ❌ Common Tanglish Phrase | ✅ Polished Global English | Why the Indian Form Sounds Unnatural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"I told to him"* | **"I told him"** | "Tell" takes a direct personal object without the preposition "to". |
| *"We discussed about the bug"* | **"We discussed the bug"** | "Discuss" already means "talk about"; adding "about" is redundant. |
| *"Today only I came"* | **"I arrived today"** | Overuse of "only" (from Tamil *-dhan* / இன்னைக்குதான்) is grammatically incorrect. |
| *"Please revert back"* | **"Please reply / get back to me"** | In global English, "revert" means Git rollback or return to an ancestral state. |
| *"Can we prepone the call?"* | **"Can we move up / reschedule earlier?"** | "Prepone" is Indian English; international clients use "move up". |
| *"I am out of station"* | **"I am out of town / traveling"** | "Out of station" is British-colonial Indian railway slang; say "out of town". |
| *"Myself Vivek"* | **"I'm Vivek / My name is Vivek"** | "Myself" is a reflexive pronoun and cannot legally serve as the subject. |
| *"Did you had food?"* | **"Did you have lunch / Have you eaten?"** | Auxiliary "Did" takes the base verb ("have"), never past tense ("had"). |
| *"He did a mistake"* | **"He made a mistake"** | Collocation rule: You *make* a mistake, you don't *do* one. |
| *"I gave an exam"* | **"I took / sat for an exam"** | In English, teachers *give* exams; students *take* exams! |
